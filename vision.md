# Impulse: Vision Document

## The Core Fantasy

You command the outer system rebellion. Earth and its inner system allies have overwhelming force - massive navies, dense orbital defenses, industrial capacity that dwarfs yours by orders of magnitude. They don't need cleverness. They need presence.

You can't win a straight fight. But you don't have to.

Your ships run hotter. Antimatter drives where they have fusion. Heat dissipation tech that lets you sustain burns that would cook their crews. You're faster, more maneuverable, and you can cross the vast distances of the outer system in weeks instead of months.

You raid. You hit supply lines. You pick your engagements and vanish before their fleets can concentrate. You're the insurgency in the void, and the void is very, very large.

---

## The Feel of Play

### Time as a Resource

The simulation runs at 60x minimum. Minutes become seconds. A missile salvo crossing 10,000km takes 16 real seconds to arrive. Close combat at 1,000km happens in under two seconds of real time.

You have a limited pool of bullet-time - moments where you can slow below 60x, where you can breathe and think. This pool is precious. It regenerates slowly. Victories grant more. Losses drain it.

When you're flush with bullet-time, you can micromanage critical moments. When you're running dry, you're forced to trust your doctrine and watch events unfold at speed. A fleet that's losing has less ability to think clearly - exactly when they need it most. Panic is real.

This creates a distinctive rhythm: long stretches of compressed time where you monitor and trust your automation, punctuated by intense moments where you slam into bullet-time because something unexpected happened and you need to *think*.

### Commander, Not Gunner

You don't fire individual weapons. You don't pilot missiles. You set doctrine:

- Point defense priorities: protect this ship, guard this cone of space, conserve interceptors, engage at this range
- Offensive targeting: which targets, what timing, attack vectors
- Fleet behavior: formation, engagement distance, conditions for withdrawal

Your skill expresses through preparation. A veteran player sets good defaults and spends bullet-time only at true decision points. A novice spends it constantly and runs dry at the worst moment.

When your doctrine is right, you can survive at 60x with minimal intervention. When your doctrine is wrong for the situation, you either spend bullet-time to override or watch your ships execute the wrong plan.

## The Asymmetry

### Inner System (AI-controlled)

- **Overwhelming force**: Larger fleets, more ships, deeper reserves
- **Dense defenses**: Orbital platforms, layered point defense, established infrastructure
- **Shorter supply lines**: Can resupply and reinforce quickly
- **Brute force doctrine**: They don't need to outmaneuver you. They need to find you and concentrate force.

This makes them predictable - which is good for AI behavior. They're not stupid, but they're not scrappy either.

### Outer System (Player)

- **Antimatter advantage**: Higher specific impulse, better thrust-to-weight for small ships. Missiles and strike craft that outperform their fusion-driven equivalents.
- **Heat dissipation**: Can sustain burns that would force inner system ships to throttle back. Enables both tactical maneuver and strategic mobility.
- **Speed and choice**: You decide when and where to fight. They react to you.
- **Scarcity**: Less of everything. Every ship matters. Every engagement has to count.

The outer system plays like an insurgency: raid logistics, avoid pitched battles, attrit their will to project power across the vast distances of the outer solar system.

---

## The Tensions

### Bullet-Time Scarcity vs Engagement Distance

Low on bullet-time? You want long-range engagements where 60x is still manageable. But long range favors the inner system's missile superiority. Bullet-time poverty pushes you toward tactically unfavorable positions.

### Doctrine vs Improvisation

You pre-commit to behaviors before combat. But circumstances change. Do you have the bullet-time to notice and override? Or do you trust your past self's judgment and let it play out?

### Heat Advantage vs Tempo Compression

Your ships want to close distance where heat tolerance matters. But closing distance compresses decision time. You're trading strategic advantage for temporal pressure.

### Commander Risk

If bullet-time is tied to commander capability, where does the commander sit? Flagship in the rear is safer but a single point of failure. Distributed command is resilient but less effective.

### Momentum and Morale

Victories grant bullet-time. Losses drain it. A winning fleet thinks more clearly; a losing fleet panics. Recovery means retreat, regrouping, letting time heal. You can't push through on willpower alone.

## The Strategic Layer

### Topography of the Void

Space has geography. Lagrange points are strategic locations - stable positions, natural waypoints. Low-energy manifolds are the highways of bulk freight - and therefore juicy targets for raids, which necessitates patrols, which creates opportunities for ambush.

The outer system's vastness is both your shield and your burden. Inner system can't project power easily across those distances. But neither can you concentrate force or resupply quickly.

### Logistics as Warfare

Civilian freighters move goods along manifold routes. Your military needs supplies. Protecting your trade routes and raiding theirs is how you win economically, not just militarily. Strangling their logistics might matter more than destroying their warships.

### The Win Condition

Survival is the baseline. How long can you hold out against overwhelming force?

But victory is possible. Earth has defenses, but they're not infinite. Wear them down over a campaign of raids and attrition. Accumulate antimatter. And when the moment is right - a MIRV strike with antimatter warheads, slipping through degraded defenses.

Very hard. But possible. The kill-shot exists if you can earn it.

## Combat Model

### Weapons

**Missiles**: 8-15 km/s delta-v. Maneuverable, can course-correct, partially evade point defense. Easier to track with sensors. The primary offensive weapon at range.

**Coilguns**: 10-30 km/s projectile velocity depending on mass. No maneuver capability, harder to track. Effective at close range where targets can't evade. Point defense coilguns for anti-missile work.

**Point Defense**: Smaller coilguns and interceptor missiles. Automated under doctrine. Protects against incoming ordnance.

### Engagement Ranges

- **100,000km**: Exploratory salvos. Missiles have time to be tracked, evaded, intercepted. Probing for weakness.
- **10,000km**: Deadly missile range. Salvos arrive fast, maneuvering matters, point defense is stressed.
- **1,000km**: Coilguns become viable. Missiles can't build full speed but have excess delta-v for terminal maneuvers. Close combat is brutal and fast.

### Force Composition

Large ships stay in the rear: sensor arrays, missile magazines, power generation. They beam power forward to smaller platforms.

Small ships and weapons platforms push forward: multi-angle offense, distributed defense, expendable if necessary. Better to lose a weapons platform than a capital ship.

This creates interesting decisions about force composition, positioning, and what you're willing to sacrifice.

---

## What Makes This Different

This isn't "RTS but in space" or "4X but harder."

The 60x floor changes everything. You can't micro your way to victory. You prepare, you set doctrine, you commit - and then you watch it play out, intervening only when you can afford to.

The asymmetry isn't a balance problem to solve - it's the game. Being the underdog is the fantasy. David vs Goliath, but David has antimatter and knows how to use the dark.

The strategic layer has real geography despite being set in space. Manifolds, Lagrange points, the tyranny of distance - these create terrain without terrain.

And the bullet-time economy creates a resource unlike anything else: your own ability to think under pressure, rationed and precious, drained by failure and replenished by success.
